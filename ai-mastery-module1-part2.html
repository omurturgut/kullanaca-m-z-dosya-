Not Just Prompts

Two people can use the same model and still produce images from different worlds. The difference is never one secret word. It is the presence or absence of a system.

When your prompt is vague, the refinement drifts. When your prompt is structured, the refinement becomes predictable.

Engine One

Structure

The Technical Skeleton

Structure answers a simple question: If a real crew tried to shoot this, how would they set it up?

Most weak prompts only describe the object. Strong prompts describe the shot.

The Structure Checklist

  • Subject & Action: Who is doing what? Which precise moment?
  • Camera: Focal length (Wide/Tele), Shot size (Close-up), Angle.
  • Lighting: Source, Direction, Quality (Soft/Hard), Temp.
  • Materials: Texture visibility, surface reflectivity.
  • Composition: Foreground/Background, Leading lines.
Engine Two

Style

Anchoring Images in Lineage

Structure makes an image believable. Style makes it recognizable.

Visual Traditions

You are not copying one image. You are borrowing language from a lineage.

  • Classic film stills
  • Analog sports photography
  • Art movements (Minimalism, Noir)

Consistency Tools

How do we lock the look?

  • Style Reference Images (sref)
  • Parameter sliders (strength)
  • Consistent palettes & contrast rules

Vision Questions

Vision turns technical facts into designed experiences.

1. What should someone feel in the first second?

2. What story beat does this frame carry?

3. How do structure and style support that feeling?

Engine Three

Vision

Turning Choices Into Emotion

Structure and style produce clean images. Vision turns them into communication.

Example: Making an image "dreamlike" by removing detail, or "documentary" by pushing contrast and grain.

Training Your Eye

AI is a laboratory where you can run thousands of experiments in minutes.

The Loop Method

Pick a strong reference. Generate your own version. Place them side-by-side. Ask: Why does theirs work better?

Weeks 1-2: Detection

You start to notice when a generation actually matches the quality of a reference.

Weeks 3-4: Diagnosis

You can name concrete reasons (silhouette, palette, light) why an image fails.

Week 5+: Prediction

You can predict which prompt decisions will work before you even press generate.

The Framework

Over time, you won't think "Three Engines". You just won't press generate until you've decided.

1. Define Structure

Subject, Camera, Light, Material, Composition.

2. Choose Style

Visual tradition, references, consistent logic.

3. State Vision

The feeling, the story, the technical support.
